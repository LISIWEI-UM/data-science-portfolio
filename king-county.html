King County house prices

How much does living area tell us about a sale price, and what do other property features add?

Open the code and results

Size explains part of the spread

I grouped 21,613 sales by living area. The smallest tenth had a median sale price of $290,000 at a median area of 940 square feet. In the largest tenth, those figures were $948,000 and 3,760 square feet. Even within the largest group the middle half of prices ranged from $750,000 to $1,365,000.

Three models on the same sales

I kept the original assignment’s 85/15 split, estimated missing values and scaling parameters from the training rows, then evaluated each model on the same 3,242 sales. Living area alone gave a test R² of 0.491. With 11 property features, linear Ridge reached 0.648. Adding squared terms and interactions raised it to 0.712, with a mean absolute error of $118,490.

The random split includes 38 property IDs on both sides, and the sales span only May 2014 to May 2015. A stronger next test would reserve later sales and keep their properties out of training.

From course notebook to portfolio

I completed IBM Skills Network’s House Sales in King County, USA lab for Assignment 01. My original notebook used the lab’s preprocessing and obtained 0.700 for degree-two Ridge. This portfolio script adds training-only imputation and standardization, compares three models and regenerates both figures.

The upstream Kaggle listing identifies the original data as CC0. The script downloads and checks IBM’s modified teaching CSV. The repository contains the code and aggregate output. Sources, method and run instructions
